# Undated All-in-One Digital Planner Standard

This is the permanent inside-planner reference for Adrianna's planner projects. Apply the structure and overall visual language to planner skills while creating original themed artwork. Do not copy the source planner's branding, copyright notice, exact icons, or dated 2025 content.

Before creating or reviewing planner pages, inspect `../assets/planner-visual-reference.jpg`. Use it as the direct visual benchmark for rounded tab shapes, the continuous top rail, slim right-edge tabs, white page base, gray typography, compact grid alignment, soft color fills, Index density, Quick Menu button proportions, monthly calendar construction, and functional-page spacing.

## Required intake before generation

Ask these before creating the cover, master shell, or pages:

1. What theme do you want for the planner?
2. What colors do you want for the tabs?

If one answer is already present, confirm it and ask only for the missing answer. Also establish page count, dimensions, dated or undated status, required sections, tab labels, and output type. Standard planner size is 567 x 726 px unless the user requests otherwise. Phone planners retain 1080 x 1920 px.

## Overall visual character

- Premium minimal digital-planner appearance, not a scrapbook worksheet floating on a background.
- White or soft-cream full-canvas interior with very pale gray dividers and borders.
- Soft theme colors used as light fills, section headers, active states, and tab colors.
- Clean modern sans-serif type, medium-gray text, small functional headings, and strong alignment.
- Compact grid-based layouts with thin rules, small labels, consistent padding, and generous usable writing areas.
- Interior decoration stays subtle and near edges; functional structure is the focal point.
- About 60-75% of functional pages is writable or interactive space; decoration is generally 10-15% or less.
- Reduce decoration, title size, padding, and border thickness before removing useful sections or writing space.

The user's selected theme changes the palette, icons, small edge decorations, cover art, and personality. It does not change the navigation geometry, compactness, usefulness, or clean finish unless explicitly requested.

## Cover rule

- Every digital-planner cover has a deliberate left side margin or slim spine strip inside the canvas.
- Coordinate the cover margin with the chosen tab colors.
- Do not add rings, discs, or spirals unless requested.
- Keep the title balanced, readable, and fully inside the canvas.
- Closed covers normally do not show working interior tabs.
- Only the cover receives the side-margin treatment. Interior pages are full-canvas with no exterior side margin, mat, mockup surface, floating page, black bar, or outer drop shadow.

## Master interior shell and tab geometry

Create one shell and reuse it for every interior page.

### Top navigation tabs

- Form a continuous row across the top edge.
- Use compact tab panels with straight bottom edges and strongly rounded upper-left and upper-right corners.
- Keep every tab equal in height with consistent widths, spacing, baseline, outline, and typography.
- Begin with text tabs for `INDEX`, `QUICK MENU`, and `CUSTOM NOTES` when those destinations exist.
- Follow with compact icon tabs for Home and major sections such as Lifestyle, Wellness, Fitness, Productivity, Finance, and Notebooks.
- Use thin monochrome line icons centered in the panels.
- Active tab may become white or use a stronger version of its assigned color; inactive tabs use light theme colors.
- Top tabs remain fully inside the canvas and occupy a slim navigation band rather than a large header.

### Right-edge tabs

- Use a single vertical stack attached to the right page edge.
- Shape each as a slim rounded rectangle with the inner edge visually joined to the page and the two outer corners softly rounded.
- Keep every right tab equal in width and height with consistent small gaps.
- Place labels vertically and center them precisely.
- Standard all-in-one sequence: `YEAR`, `JAN`, `FEB`, `MAR`, `APR`, `MAY`, `JUN`, `JUL`, `AUG`, `SEP`, `OCT`, `NOV`, `DEC`.
- For undated planners, keep month names but omit the year number. For dated planners, use only the user-approved year.
- For non-calendar planners, replace month labels with the approved section sequence while preserving the same shape and geometry.
- Apply the user's chosen tab colors in a coordinated repeating sequence.

### Lock rules

After shell approval, never change tab count, labels, order, shape, radius, size, position, gaps, font, icons, color sequence, active state treatment, or alignment from page to page. Edit only the central content-safe area.

## Navigation and dashboard pages

Use these when the page count and product scope support them:

1. Index: grouped multi-column table of contents with every included page title and clickable destination.
2. Quick Menu: large 3-column grid of rounded-square buttons with simple icons and labels for commonly used pages.
3. Year Overview: compact 12-month overview plus a small notes area.
4. Month at a Glance: 12 clickable month buttons in a compact grid.
5. Monthly Planner Index: month rows linked to each month and its weekly pages.
6. Monthly Review Index: 12 month buttons linked to monthly reflection pages.
7. Custom Notes: notebook or note-style destination with selectable layouts when included.
8. Favorite Stickers: optional sticker-access destination.

Quick Menu should prioritize To Do List, Goal Planner, Project Planner, Expense Tracker, Daily Routine, Study Session, Habit Tracker, Mood Tracker, Meal Planner, Thoughts & Ideas, Grocery List, and Favorite Stickers when those pages are present.

## Core calendar system

Default to undated unless the user requests a year.

- Year overview with 12 compact mini calendars or month blocks.
- One monthly calendar for each month with a Monday-start grid when the user requests Monday start.
- Each month page includes useful compact areas below or beside the calendar, such as To Do, Goals, Notes, Priorities, or Important Dates.
- Weekly navigation supports W1-W5 and W6 where needed.
- Weekly pages use a clean seven-day layout plus compact priorities, tasks, notes, habits, or meal areas.
- Monthly review pages include Wins, Challenges, Lessons, Progress, Gratitude, and Next-Month Focus when space allows.
- Daily pages use a compact schedule, Top 3, task list, notes, meals, water, mood, and self-care areas as appropriate.

## Functional layout rules

- Give each worksheet 4-7 useful sections when the page type supports it.
- Use alternating faint row fills, small color-coded headers, checkboxes, lines, compact tables, trackers, grids, and columns.
- Avoid one huge box that only supports one or two entries.
- Keep labels short and legible.
- Use a strong alignment grid so section edges, baselines, and padding match.
- Reserve the top navigation band and right-tab rail before laying out content.
- Never cover, crop, or push content behind a tab.

## Hyperlink system

Add real invisible PDF link annotations aligned to visible controls.

- Every top tab links to its destination.
- Every right-edge month or section tab links to its divider or dashboard.
- Every Index entry links to the exact page type.
- Every Quick Menu button links to its exact page.
- Home, Back, Previous, Next, monthly buttons, W1-W6 buttons, and monthly review buttons link to valid destinations.
- Repeat the same link rectangles on every page that repeats the same navigation shell.
- Active tabs remain clickable unless the viewer or user requirement says otherwise.
- Validate that every destination exists, every rectangle is inside the page, link borders are invisible, and no overlapping rectangles point to conflicting destinations.

## Completion and quality control

- Create and count a full page manifest before generation.
- Generate every requested page separately at the approved dimensions.
- Do not count a blank shell or mockup as a functional page.
- Continue through the entire manifest rather than stopping at a cover, shell, or first batch.
- Check exact spelling, page order, consistent tabs, consistent navigation, writing space, margins, and visual density.
- Assemble and hyperlink the final PDF only after artwork is approved.
- Render representative pages and all navigation families for visual inspection.
- Test annotation count, destinations, repeated controls, and page-boundary compliance before delivery.
